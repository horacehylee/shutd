Shutd tray icon concept

All variants are monochrome and rendered at runtime by tiny-skia onto a 32×32 RGBA buffer, one glyph color picked from the SystemUsesLightTheme registry value: near-black on a light taskbar, white on a dark taskbar. The SVGs below approximate exactly what the rasterizer draws: stroked circles and arc segments only, so every variant is a handful of path strokes in Rust. States differ by geometry, never by color, because the Windows 11 tray is monochrome by convention.

Option A — Power glyph, depleting outer ring

The ring is the fraction of the day left until the next shutdown, so it drains from full at midnight to empty at the shutdown time. Inside the notification window the glyph stays the same; the panel and toast carry the urgency. The ring gives the tray a live signal without extra color, matching the OpenQuota gauge pattern.

Option B — Power glyph only, 2.0 stroke (chosen)

The same glyph with slightly heavier strokes and no ring. Simplest to draw and the quietest in the tray, but it looks identical all day, so shutdown proximity is invisible until the panel opens.

Option C — Ring only

Pure gauge, closest to the OpenQuota ring. Cleanest at 16 px, but it reads as a generic meter rather than shutdown, and it loses the power identity the app has today.

Against today's icon

The current tray icon is a magenta power glyph. Color breaks the monochrome tray convention, and the glyph geometry is heavier than the Windows 11 system icons next to it. The proposals keep the power identity but render in the taskbar's own foreground color, so the icon sits quietly between volume and network regardless of theme.
